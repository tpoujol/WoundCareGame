# Welcome to wound healing

Wound healing is the body's coordinated repair adventure. Every dressing choice, cleansing step, and clinical observation supports a living sequence of cellular teamwork.

> Think of healing as a relay race: each phase passes the baton to the next so the wound can move from emergency response to strong, remodelled tissue.

## Phase 1: Hemostasis — stop the leak

Hemostasis begins immediately after injury. Blood vessels constrict, platelets gather, and a fibrin clot forms a temporary plug. This clot limits blood loss and creates an early scaffold for incoming repair cells.

**Game cue:** this is the shield phase. The body protects the area and sets the stage.

## Phase 2: Inflammation — clean and signal

Inflammation brings immune cells to remove debris, bacteria, and damaged tissue. Redness, warmth, swelling, and some pain can be normal early signals, but excessive or persistent inflammation may delay progress.

**Game cue:** this is the clean-up crew. The wound needs enough activity to reset the environment, but not so much that healing stalls.

## Phase 3: Proliferation — rebuild the base

During proliferation, fibroblasts create extracellular matrix, new blood vessels support oxygen delivery, and granulation tissue fills the wound. Epithelial cells migrate from the edges to resurface the wound.

**Game cue:** this is the construction phase. Healthy red granulation and edge advancement are encouraging signs.

## Phase 4: Maturation — strengthen and remodel

Maturation can continue for months. Collagen reorganises, tensile strength improves, and scar tissue becomes more stable. The healed area is stronger than before, although it may not regain full original strength.

**Game cue:** this is the upgrade phase. The repair becomes more organised and resilient.

## Why MMP balance matters

Matrix metalloproteinases, often shortened to **MMPs**, are enzymes that help remodel extracellular matrix. In the right amount, they clear damaged matrix and make room for new tissue. In chronic wounds, MMP activity can become excessive and break down growth factors, receptors, and fresh matrix that the wound needs to progress.

That means MMPs are not villains; they are powerful tools that need balance. Good wound care aims to support a wound environment where protease activity, moisture, bacterial burden, oxygen, and tissue health are all moving in the right direction.

## Quick win checklist

- Identify the current healing phase and whether the wound appears stuck.
- Look for tissue type: necrotic, sloughy, granulating, epithelialising, or hypergranulating.
- Watch for infection signs such as spreading redness, heat, swelling, pain, odour, delayed healing, or increased exudate.
- Choose dressings based on wound goals: protect, hydrate, absorb, debride, manage bacteria, or support matrix balance.
- Celebrate progress: smaller dimensions, healthier tissue, lower exudate, and advancing wound edges all matter.
